  as TerminalKit's do. The AemiRegex grammar differential tests run in the default suite: AemiRegex has a remote now, pinned by revision as a

  snapshot") was wrong; a byte-at-a-time property test over the spec examples found it (673 failures, 19 examples) and the parser's own open
  stack gives the right boundary.
- Suites: 44 + 2 tests, debug and release; ratchet empty (every CommonMark and GFM example passes); analyzers clean.
- Benchmark, fresh: a new corpus (17,001 assistant messages, 10.2 MB; the 19,846-message corpus of 2026-10-25 no longer exists as the logs changed), so
  swift-markdown (`392b73b`, swift-cmark `a7ecfef`) was measured again beside it, two runs each: 14.8 against 200 to 203 MB/s (272 to 278 reused); 252.5
  against 35.2 allocations per KB (0.2 reused); a 100 KB streamed answer 4.07 to 4.10 s against 0.005 s. Clocks differ (wall against thread CPU), noted
  in the README. The 100 KB single paragraph is 148 to 150 µs per delta, down from 261 µs on 2026-10-25 (different corpus run, same synthetic input).

  plugin resolves only with `AEMIMARKDOWN_DEV=1`.
- README: status now says what passes; the measured table with its conditions; the licence notice lists the three differently licensed fixture files.
- The cmark comparison, run for the first time today (cmark 0.31.2 built from source, 5,000 random inputs): 16 differ. 12 had one cause: cmark keeps a
  lazy continuation line's leading whitespace (it adds the line from where container matching stopped), which shows in code spans and after a backslash
  break. Fixed (the lazy line keeps it; the inline phase drops it where cmark does); `LazyContinuationTests` hold cmark's output and fail without the fix.
  The other 4 are cmark's own (a title the spec says is not there; entity-before-escape in info strings), documented in `DifferentialTests` and not
  followed. After: 4 of 5,000 and 2 of 20,000 (seed 2), all of those two kinds. Throughput and allocations unchanged (201 to 203 MB/s, 35.2 per KB).
- Walking one block allocated two (the stacks reserved up front, not per node). `MarkdownEvents.restart(at:in:)` reuses one walker: zero per walk once
  warm, in the allocation suite. Reserving lazily instead was measured and rejected: nested blocks regrew their stacks (a list 4, a table 3).
- The GFM comparison against cmark-gfm 0.29.0.gfm.13, first run: the script ran cmark-gfm without task lists and footnotes, which `.gfm` turns on (584 of
  5,000 differed, then every input when `--footnotes` turned out not to be a flag: it is `-e footnotes`). Aligned: 286, then 278 after fixing the one real
  fault, a task marker with nothing after it (`- [x]` is text; `TaskMarkerTests` hold cmark-gfm's output). The rest is classified in `DifferentialTests`:
  cmark-gfm's own (150 + 68), spec 0.29 and block-level task markers (44), one split tab, and 15 owed (bare-link extent through `\*` or a backtick).
- The benchmark harness trapped on a corpus with no multi-line message (it indexed an empty stream); it now skips that stream.
